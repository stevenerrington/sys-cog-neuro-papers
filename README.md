# Systems & Cognitive Neuro Digest

**→ [stevenerrington.github.io/sys-cog-neuro-papers](https://stevenerrington.github.io/sys-cog-neuro-papers/)**

A daily digest of new papers in systems and cognitive neuroscience on **auditory
processing, predictive coding, memory and sequences**. Each morning it collects new
preprints from bioRxiv and new articles from 89 journals, scores each one for relevance,
and ranks the list so the most relevant papers appear first. Work in macaques and
marmosets is weighted highest, followed by human and rodent studies.

The site shows a rolling 30-day window and updates every day at about 06:00 UK time.

---

## Using the site

- **Ranked list.** Papers are ordered by relevance score by default. Switch to *Newest*
  to sort by date instead.
- **Filters.** Narrow the list by topic, species, method, journal group, individual
  journal, source (preprints or journals) and how recently the paper was posted (7, 14
  or 30 days). *Any selected* shows papers that match at least one filter; *All
  selected* shows only papers that match every filter.
- **Search.** Matches words in titles, abstracts and author names.
- **New papers.** Papers added in the latest update are labelled **new**. Tick *New
  since last update only* to see just those.
- **Abstract & why it ranked.** Opens a paper's abstract with the matched terms
  highlighted, along with a breakdown of its score.

## How papers are ranked

Each paper's title and abstract are matched against keywords for every topic:

- **Topics.** A match in the title counts three times as much as a match in the
  abstract. Each topic has a weight, so specific topics count more than broad ones.
  For example, *local–global* and *non-adjacent dependencies* are weighted higher than
  general *memory*.
- **Methods.** Single-unit, LFP and fMRI studies get the largest bonus, followed by
  intracranial and EEG/MEG work.
- **Species.** The total is then multiplied by species: macaque and marmoset ×2, other
  primates ×1.6, human ×1.25 and rodent ×1.15.

Papers below a minimum score are left out. Open any paper's abstract to see exactly
how its score was reached.

## Things to bear in mind

- **Tagging is based on keywords.** A paper that doesn't use any of the listed terms
  won't be picked up, however relevant it is. A study is tagged *Macaque* only if it
  says macaque or rhesus. Papers that only say "monkey" are tagged *Other primate*.
- **Journals marked † often have no abstract** in Crossref, so they are ranked on their
  titles alone and tend to appear lower.
- **Dates are publication dates** as reported by each source. A paper can appear a few
  days after it was published, because indexing sometimes lags.
- **The score measures relevance to these topics, not quality or impact.**
